The Ecosystem

The six pillars have distinct jobs. They share approved information, tools, and lessons so each project can build on the others’ work while personal records remain private.

  • SOURCE
  • NuMind
  • VATRA
  • Deus Ordo
  • VIO
  • FamilyOS

SOURCE

Information

Source is designed to collect information from accessible feeds, APIs, public databases, and websites. It keeps citations and a record of changes so NuMind and VIO can check where information came from. The design calls for deduplication, access controls, distributed storage, and tested backups.

Visit SOURCE and open the sample records

NuMind

Knowledge

NuMind aims to turn source material into lessons, manuals, and blueprints people can use. The plan covers fields from science and engineering to agriculture and construction, with resources that can eventually be read offline, translated, and printed.

Visit NuMind

VATRA

Society

VATRA is planning a community that can provide more of its own essentials. The work starts with questions about land, housing, water, power, food, and how families would share responsibility for them.

Visit VATRA

Deus Ordo

People

DEUS ORDO brings men together for mentorship, accountability, and community service. It is organizing its first gathering. Over time, local chapters could take on practical community projects and help families prepare for emergencies.

Visit DEUS ORDO

VIO

Technology

VIO focuses on local computing, AI, automation, and engineering. It starts with tools people can run, test, and maintain, from predictable scripts to systems that help make sense of information.

Visit VIO

FamilyOS

Private household

FamilyOS is the private part of the NuTerra ecosystem. It is designed to give each person a place for their own records, routines, and progress, with a shared household space for information they choose to share.

The current prototype supports shared household planning. Individual record permissions are planned.

Open the FamilyOS guide with fictional records

The other pillars share public guides, tools, and project findings. The FamilyOS design keeps personal records private by default. Sharing personal records with another pillar would require explicit approval for that purpose.
