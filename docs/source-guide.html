Public evidence guide

Create a traceable record for a public claim, document the review, and verify that an export restores intact.

In order
  1. Capture the claim and origin
  2. Check the scope of the evidence
  3. Price and publish a reviewable batch
  4. Verify the export and restore it
  5. Maintain and upgrade the catalog

1. Capture the claim and origin

One record should state one checkable claim. Assign a stable record ID and keep the original URL, publisher, title, edition or date, retrieval date, applicable rights, and the part that supports the claim. Record any privacy restriction before adding the item. Prioritize portable repair manuals, emergency references, offline maps, and records of what actually worked. Track each edition, jurisdiction where relevant, permissions, and whether a second person could open it offline.

W3C provenance model · Download sample records

2. Check the scope of the evidence

Write what the source establishes, what observation you made, and what remains unknown. For example, the Kiwix publisher explains how to open a ZIM offline. A device-specific test would separately record the device, archive version, test date, and observed behavior. Kiwix’s documentation

3. Price and publish a reviewable batch

Estimate reviewer time separately from storage. For 12 records at 5 minutes each, review time is 60 minutes. Record the review status, reviewer role, date, and a short justification. Export only material cleared for sharing. Assign corrections their own version and link both versions.

4. Verify the export and restore it

Export a UTF-8 CSV or structured data file and keep a SHA-256 manifest for the files permitted in the package. Restore a clean copy, compare file hashes, and open a sample row and its evidence. Check that every expected file is present and matches. A restore with 9 matching files out of 10 fails the integrity check.

BagIt file packaging · NDSA preservation guidance

Try with the sample CSV. Save the sample records and checksum into the same folder. Open a terminal there and run shasum -a 256 -c source-example.sha256 on macOS or sha256sum -c source-example.sha256 on a system with GNU Coreutils. Open the checked CSV and find each original publisher. Keep a clean copy and change a single character in a disposable copy to see the check fail.

5. Maintain and upgrade the catalog

Review stale links and disputed claims. Record a source’s replacement or loss of access. Keep dated export snapshots, check restore after tooling changes, and keep correction history readable. Use a standard package when exchange with another curator warrants it.
